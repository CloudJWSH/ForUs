Glossary

The words around pump.fun.

What the panel shows and how pump.fun prices trades, in short. The numbers are the starting values of a new pump.fun curve.

Bonding curve
The pricing formula every new pump.fun coin starts on. Instead of an order book there are two reserves, SOL and tokens, and their product stays constant. When you buy, SOL goes in and tokens come out, so the price rises. A new curve starts at 30 SOL against 1.073 bn tokens. Pump Paper reads these reserves straight from the blockchain.
Curve progress
How much of the roughly 793 M tokens sold through the curve are gone. The further along the curve, the more each further token costs.
Migration
When the curve is full, the coin moves from the curve into a pool. Many call it graduation. Pump Paper detects the switch and prices with the pool from then on, including positions you still hold.
PumpSwap pool
pump.fun's market after migration. The same two-reserve formula applies, with real instead of virtual reserves and its own fee.
Market cap (MC)
The price of one token times one billion, the fixed supply of every pump.fun coin. The panel shows it in dollars and in SOL. Pump Paper converts target and stop into market cap too, so you can compare them with the chart.
SOL and USDC coins
Most coins trade against SOL, some against USDC. Pump Paper detects the currency per coin. You pay USDC coins from your USDC balance and everything else from SOL.
Fee
pump.fun keeps a share of every trade. On the curve that is the protocol and creator fee, in the pool a share for the pool is added. The rate depends on market cap. Pump Paper reads it from pump.fun's fee config. If that is missing it uses 1.25 % on the curve (0.95 + 0.30).
Priority fee
An extra payment to the Solana network so your transaction lands in a block sooner. It is charged even when the trade fails. In Pump Paper you set it in the options, default 0.001 SOL.
Slippage
How much worse the price may be at execution than at your click. If the gap is larger, pump.fun cancels the trade, and so does Pump Paper. Try it in the calculator.
Delay
Time passes between your click and the block your trade lands in. On hot coins others buy ahead of you in that time. Pump Paper therefore fills only after an adjustable delay, default 1 s, at the price that holds then.
Account rent
To hold a token your wallet needs its own token account. For that Solana sets aside about 0.00204 SOL on the first buy. Pump Paper deducts this amount on the first buy of a coin.
Solana endpoint (RPC)
The server Pump Paper reads prices and fees from. The public endpoint is fine to start. Your own, for example from Helius, is faster. If one doesn't answer, Pump Paper takes a second one.
Target, stop, time
Rules for selling automatically: at a profit, at a loss or after a time. A stop sells without a slippage limit and lands below the mark in a crash. How far is shown by the stop calculator.
Paper trading
Trading with play money under real conditions. It is only worth something if it prices like the real thing. Otherwise you practice with profits that would never have existed.
Hit rate
Share of sells that made a profit. On its own it says little: a 30 % hit rate can be enough if the winners are much bigger than the losers.
Profit factor
All profits divided by all losses. Above 1 you make money overall, below it you lose.
Drawdown
The largest drop of the account from a high to the next low. It shows how much you have to sit through along the way.
Expectancy
What a trade makes on average: hit rate times average win minus loss rate times average loss. If it is negative, more trades won't help.
Rug pull
Developers or large holders sell all at once and the price collapses. On the curve nobody can pull the liquidity, but a large sell still pushes the price down hard.
Bundle
Several buys from different wallets in the same block the coin is created in, often by the same people. It makes the developers' share look smaller than it is.
Sniper
Bots that buy in the first seconds after launch and often sell again quickly. Their selling can make an entry shortly after expensive.
Dev wallet
The wallet that created the coin. Anyone can see on Solscan how much it holds and whether it sells. A large share is a risk for every other holder.
Creator fee
The part of every trade's fee that goes to the coin's creator. On the curve it is 0.30 % of the 1.25 %.
FDV
Fully diluted valuation: price times total supply. pump.fun coins have a fixed supply of one billion, so FDV and market cap are the same.
