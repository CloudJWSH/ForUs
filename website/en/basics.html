What a strategy is

A strategy is three rules you set before you see a coin:

  • Entry: Which coins qualify? For example by market cap, curve progress and age.
  • Exit: When do you sell? With target, stop and time, and if you like a trailing stop or partial sell.
  • Size: How much per trade? Fixed or a share of the account, see position size.

Deciding during the trade means following your gut. Written rules can be tested, compared and improved.

Costs first

Every trade costs, however it ends. On the curve that's about 1.25% fee on the buy and again on the sell, plus the priority fee per trade and, on the first buy of a coin, the account rent.

An example with 0.1 SOL size and the default values: 0.0025 SOL fee, 0.002 SOL priority fee for buy and sell, 0.00204 SOL rent. Together about 0.0065 SOL, roughly 6.5%. The coin has to rise by that much before you are even at zero. Small sizes are expensive in relative terms.

Numbers that matter

The hit rate alone says little. More important is the expectancy: what a trade brings on average, after costs. Add the drawdown, how deep it goes in between, and the equity curve, which shows whether a result builds steadily or hangs on one lucky hit.

What a test can't do

  • A backtest in the lab runs on made-up curves. It shows whether your rules are consistent, not what real coins do tomorrow.
  • Few trades are luck. Below about 20 trades a sample is too small.
  • Turning the knobs until the test shines is overfitting. Test with several seeds.
  • Paper is practice. Even a good paper result is no guarantee for real money.
Pump Paper never buys on its own. Your strategy helps your decision. You make the buy, with play money.
